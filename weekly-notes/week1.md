# Week 1 Progress Report

## Project

Exploring the Genomic Basis of Nutritional and Medicinal Traits in *Moringa oleifera*: A Bioinformatics Investigation

## Reflection

This week marked the beginning of my first structured bioinformatics investigation focused on *Moringa oleifera*. My primary goal was not to identify genes immediately but to develop a solid understanding of the nutritional, medicinal, and genomic background of the species.

Through reviewing scientific literature and exploring genomic databases, I gained a better appreciation of how bioinformatics can be used to connect biological traits with the genes and pathways that may influence them.

## What I Accomplished

During Week 1, I:

- Developed the project proposal and research objectives.
- Conducted a literature review on the nutritional properties of *Moringa oleifera*.
- Conducted a literature review on the medicinal properties of *Moringa oleifera*.
- Identified major bioactive compounds reported in the literature.
- Explored available genomic and transcriptomic resources.
- Selected a reference genome for future analyses.
- Retrieved a leaf transcriptomic dataset for further investigation.
- Created and organized the GitHub repository structure.

## Key Insights

One of the most interesting observations from the literature is that many of the reported nutritional and medicinal properties of *Moringa oleifera* appear to be linked to a relatively small number of important biochemical pathways.

Several compounds repeatedly appeared throughout the literature, including:

- Flavonoids
- Phenolic compounds
- Glucosinolates
- Isothiocyanates
- Vitamins
- Antioxidants

This suggests that investigating pathways involved in secondary metabolism may provide valuable insights into the biological basis of these traits.

## Challenges Encountered

This week was also a reminder that bioinformatics involves much more than running software tools.

Some challenges included:

- Learning how genomic datasets are organized within NCBI.
- Understanding the differences between genome assemblies, annotations, and transcriptomic data.
- Interpreting large genomic resource files.
- Learning how to organize scientific information for reproducible research.

## Lessons

A major lesson from this week is that successful bioinformatics projects begin with biological questions, not software tools.

The literature review helped me realize the importance of understanding:

- What trait is being studied.
- Which compounds are involved.
- Which pathways produce those compounds.
- Which genes may regulate those pathways.
  
This framework will guide the next phase of the project.

This week established the scientific foundation that will support the bioinformatics investigations to follow.
