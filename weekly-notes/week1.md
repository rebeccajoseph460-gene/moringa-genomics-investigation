This week marked the start of my first structured bioinformatics project focused on *Moringa oleifera*. Rather than jumping straight into gene discovery, I focused on understanding the plant's nutritional, medicinal, and genomic background. This foundation was important because meaningful bioinformatics analyses begin with biological questions rather than software tools.

## What I Accomplished

During Week 1, I:

- Developed the project objectives and research plan.
- Reviewed literature on the nutritional properties of *Moringa oleifera*.
- Explored reported medicinal applications and supporting scientific evidence.
- Identified major bioactive compounds frequently discussed in the literature.
- Investigated available genomic and transcriptomic resources.
- Selected a reference genome assembly for future analyses.
- Retrieved an RNA-seq dataset from leaf tissue.
- Created and organized the GitHub repository.

## Key Insights

One observation from the literature was that many of the reported nutritional and medicinal benefits of *Moringa oleifera* appear to be linked to a relatively small number of biochemical pathways.

Compounds that appeared consistently across studies included:

- Flavonoids
- Phenolic compounds
- Glucosinolates
- Isothiocyanates
- Vitamins
- Antioxidants

This reinforced the importance of investigating genes involved in secondary metabolism and bioactive compound biosynthesis.

## Lessons Learned

The most important lesson from this week is that successful bioinformatics projects start with understanding the biology.

Before identifying candidate genes, it is important to understand:

- The biological trait of interest.
- The metabolites involved.
- The pathways that produce those metabolites.
- The genes that may regulate those pathways.

This understanding will guide the next phase of the project.
