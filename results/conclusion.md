# Conclusion

This study employed bioinformatics approaches to investigate the genomic basis of the nutritional and medicinal properties of *Moringa oleifera*. Publicly available genomic and RNA-seq datasets were analysed through a workflow involving RNA-seq alignment, structural genome annotation, functional annotation, candidate gene identification, and pathway analysis.

Structural annotation using AUGUSTUS generated 79,227 predicted coding sequences and protein sequences, providing a foundation for functional characterisation. Functional annotation using eggNOG-mapper successfully assigned biological functions, Gene Ontology terms, and KEGG pathway information to 34,675 predicted proteins.

Several candidate genes associated with nutritional traits were identified, including genes involved in mineral transport, protein biosynthesis, carbohydrate metabolism, and energy metabolism. Notable examples included a molybdate transporter, an aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase, a glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase, and a gluconokinase.

Candidate genes potentially associated with medicinal traits were also identified. These included sterol 3-beta-glucosyltransferases, glycosyltransferases, and glycosyl hydrolases, which are linked to sterol metabolism, secondary metabolite modification, and the production of biologically active compounds.

Pathway analysis revealed the involvement of candidate genes in important biological pathways, including aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis, glycerophospholipid metabolism, pentose phosphate metabolism, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, and secondary metabolite biosynthesis. These pathways provide plausible molecular explanations for the nutritional value and medicinal potential reported for *Moringa oleifera*.

Overall, the findings demonstrate the usefulness of genomics and bioinformatics approaches for identifying genes and pathways associated with important plant traits. The candidate genes identified in this study represent promising targets for future experimental validation and functional genomics research aimed at improving understanding of the nutritional and medicinal properties of *Moringa oleifera*.
